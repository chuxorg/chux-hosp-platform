# SaaS architecture quality attributes and NFR baseline

Status: **Proposed — owner review required; targets are not measured results or customer SLAs.**
Date: 2026-11-24 | Tracking: [#57](https://github.com/chuxorg/chux-hosp-platform/issues/57)
Authority: engineering baseline under [SDLC](SDLC.md); approved commercial requirements remain in the [Dropbox Hospitality workspace](https://www.dropbox.com/home/Hospitality).
Related: [ADR-0002](adr/ADR-0002-modular-hospitality-platform.md), [ADR-0003](adr/ADR-0003-go-postgres-defer-redis.md), [ADR-0004](adr/ADR-0004-configuration-driven-business-policies.md).

## Context and constraints
The platform must be capable of supporting non-paying customers at bounded marginal cost, and small paying operators at affordable prices. Hotels are the initial market, not the universal tenancy model. A tenant is the customer organization, a property is a PMS-owned operating location, a subscription describes commercial entitlements, and a deployment describes infrastructure placement. None is implicitly one-to-one. Actual pricing, free allowances, conversion, support obligations and customer volumes remain unvalidated commercial assumptions.

Propose pooled application and PostgreSQL infrastructure as the initial deployment; tenant creation must not normally provision infrastructure. Preserve Go, PostgreSQL, modular-monolith boundaries, typed policy configuration, and authoritative reservation transactions. No cloud vendor decision, microservices, Redis, broker, Kubernetes, per-tenant database, generic control plane or on-prem support commitment is made here. Bridge (separate database) and silo (dedicated runtime/database) are later options, subject to evidence and funding. One deployment cell initially; do not implement routing abstractions without a concrete story.

## Requirements and validation matrix
All quantitative thresholds are provisional. P0 invariants are proposed mandatory production gates, independent of tier; they are not assertions that current code satisfies them. P1 objectives must be validated before an operational pilot or explicitly revised by the owner. P2 is later, evidence-driven work. Stable NFR identifiers supplement, not replace, approved REQ IDs.

## Reference workload (synthetic planning fixture, not market evidence)
Start with 50 tenants / 60 properties (including multi-property operators), 30 rooms/property, 5 staff accounts/property, 70% occupancy, average stay 2 nights, 12 months of history. Generate roughly 630 room-stay arrivals/day across the fixture under those assumptions; arrivals are not API request counts. Exercise 30 concurrent staff sessions, 20 requests/sec sustained, 40 requests/sec burst; illustrative mix 70% reads, 25% non-booking writes, 5% confirmations. Include booking conflicts, repeated retries, report queries and a noisy tenant; seed only synthetic data. Test 10, 50, 100 and 250 tenant scenarios, increasing resources only when evidence justifies it. Record fixture seed, actual data volume, commit, hardware/region, DB settings, endpoint mix and concurrency. Revisit assumptions with real hotel profiles before interpreting capacity. A pilot with smaller volumes does not prove the 50-tenant target.

## Cost measurement and commercial separation
Monthly recurring infrastructure = compute + database + backups/storage + network/CDN/load balancing + identity + monitoring/logs + required third-party platform charges. Include idle capacity, minimum fees, production recovery resources and amortized recurring commitments; separately show nonproduction infrastructure, one-off setup, taxes, support labor and acquisition/development costs. Record usage-based messaging/integration expenses by tenant and avoid double-counting included allowances.

Use active account = enabled tenant with at least one authenticated operational action in the trailing 30 days; active property = property with operational activity in that interval. Also report enabled inactive accounts/properties, allocated cost and retained-data footprint. Allocate directly attributable costs first, then shared cost by documented usage drivers (e.g. DB storage, compute/request time); allocate unmeasured residual and idle overhead transparently across served cohorts. Reconcile total allocation to bill; do not divide all shared spend twice. Free cohort cost / active free properties and paying cohort cost / active paying accounts must use distinct numerators and report zero-denominator metrics as N/A. Track incremental cost of additional tenants separately from allocated average. Multi-property accounts report both units. No high-cardinality tenant labels in unrestricted metrics; use controlled aggregation with privacy/access safeguards.

Monthly dashboard: actual vs budget, cost per cohort and property/account, active/inactive counts, paid conversion, workload and p95/error rate, utilization and capacity headroom, backup/restore evidence, support hours and attributable third-party costs. Finance separately maintains price/conversion assumptions, revenue, contribution and acquisition costs. Free-tier policy must not silently delete records or disable critical ongoing stays; retention, export, suspension and quota semantics require business approval. Usage metering is not billing.

## Design/release checklist
For each affected story, cite NFR IDs and document tenant boundary, authorization and entitlements, database ownership/transaction behavior, expected workload, resource limits, cost impact, telemetry, failure behavior and test evidence. Use authoritative tenant context throughout data access; assess PostgreSQL RLS as defense-in-depth with transaction-scoped context, non-bypass runtime roles and pooled-connection leakage tests if selected. Do not assume RLS alone enforces property or feature permissions. Cross-tenant admin actions require explicit authorization and audit.

Before any real hotel data or public production endpoints: verify P0 gates; obtain explicit owner authorization, privacy/retention review, security and recovery evidence, approved production budget and operational support plan. No development-only endpoint becomes production-ready through this document. Resolve P1 misses with corrective work or explicit owner-reviewed revised objectives; never waive isolation or correctness. Keep evidence links in issues/PRs and distinguish not tested, passed, failed and not applicable. Existing CI remains mandatory and does not by itself prove these targets.

## Follow-up and open decisions
1. Review and accept/revise baseline; reconcile approved Dropbox business requirements before feature implementation.
2. Audit current tenant/property authorization and data scoping; propose bounded implementation stories for gaps.
3. Profile baseline on reproducible local/container infrastructure; compare realistic managed hosting options with same recovery/availability assumptions and dated region pricing.
4. Specify entitlements, metering, quotas and lifecycle policies as separate approved stories; avoid building generic frameworks early.
5. Establish cost/performance evidence reporting and evaluate shared capacity; revisit cell/bridge/silo only on measured saturation, contractual isolation needs or regional constraints.

Owner: project owner approves objectives/budget and production release. Engineering owns reproducible validation; operations owns monitoring, restoration and cost reconciliation (named operators TBD). Review before first real pilot, monthly during pilot, and after material workload, pricing, topology or security changes. No customer SLA, provider selection, production deployment or new business behavior is approved by this proposal.
